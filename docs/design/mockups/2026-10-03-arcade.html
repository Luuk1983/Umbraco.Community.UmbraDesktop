The Arcade, with Solitaire's flair

The Arcade's own surfaces (the hub, the result cards, the leaderboard panel, the toast) share one look taken from Solitaire: the deep navy felt lit from above, frosted glass, Fraunces for names and headings, big numerals for scores, metal medals and a crown for first place, and a soft pink shine on your own row. Restrained: no confetti, no cartoons. The games themselves keep their own look. All data is made up and consistent across the views.

1. The hub opens on where you stand

A strip with your standing across everything, then a tile per game with one row per mode. Play starts the game, the tile opens its page, your name opens your profile.

Where the excitement comes from: your own numbers, big, next to the person you are chasing.

  • A mode you never played shows "Not played yet" in place of your medal, still with the leader: an invitation.

The podium is the flair, the list is the information.
  • One job per place: the bar navigates, the header names the game and holds its one action, the pill chooses the board it sits on.
  • No separate rank and best boxes: your row says both.
  • Ranks 4 to 10 scroll under the podium; your row stays pinned at the bottom until you are in the top ten.
  • Snake has one board, so no pill.
  • Scores hidden: your row still shows to you, marked "Only you see this"; nobody else sees it.
  • Admins get a ⋯ on hover per row (remove score, reset name) and "Reset this board" at the bottom. Hidden here to keep it clean.

The card picks compact by itself from the space it is given (Snake and Solitaire below have room for the full card); a game can also ask for it. A loss shows nothing new.

How it stays a theme citizen

  • The felt, glass, pink and gold become Arcade tokens, with these values as the Umbraco look, the same way Solitaire's felt is restyled per theme. Windows 98 gets a teal felt with bevelled panels and the classic gold, macOS a graphite felt. Nothing disappears under any theme.
  • The result card and leaderboard panel are the Arcade's pieces; a game places them, and a game without them still gets the old dialog and toast.
  • Motion, small and once: the card rises in, a new-best row gets one sweep of the shine, the crown settles. Nothing loops, and all of it respects reduced motion.
